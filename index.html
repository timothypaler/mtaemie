01CHAPTER 01

Biochemistry & medicine

The molecular foundation of care.

Medicine begins deeper than a symptom: molecules, reactions, and processes inside the body. This chapter helped me understand why knowing what happens underneath a disease matters.

Molecular changes → cellular changes → organ dysfunction → clinical manifestations
02CHAPTER 02

Water & pH

The balance that makes life possible.

Water provides the environment where biochemical reactions happen. Hydrogen bonding, molecular interactions, and physiologic buffers helped me see why proper pH is essential for proteins and enzymes.

Water enables life, but pH keeps life in balance.
03CHAPTER 03

Amino acids & peptides

Small structures. Significant consequences.

The R group influences an amino acid’s properties and interactions. Joining amino acids through peptide bonds creates a sequence that influences a protein’s eventual structure and function.

The structure of a molecule determines how it behaves.
04CHAPTER 04

Primary protein structure

Reading a protein’s first story.

The amino acid sequence is more than a chain. Edman degradation, SDS-PAGE, mass spectrometry, and bioinformatics introduced me to how scientists identify and analyze proteins.

Structure gives us clues about what a protein can do.
05CHAPTER 05

Higher orders of structure

It matters how the pieces come together.

A correct amino acid sequence is only part of the story. Proteins must fold and assemble correctly, from helices and sheets to tertiary and quaternary structures.

Protein misfolding connects molecular structure with disease.
06CHAPTER 06

Myoglobin & hemoglobin

The chemistry behind every breath.

Myoglobin stores and facilitates oxygen availability in muscle, while hemoglobin transports oxygen in red blood cells. Cooperativity, the Bohr effect, and hemoglobin disorders made the clinical connection clear.

Oxygen delivery is a biochemical process.
03 / A CASE THAT CONNECTED EVERYTHINGSMALL GROUP DISCUSSION

WHEN IRON IS NOT ENOUGH

Behind every finding,
there is a story.

Henry, a five-year-old boy, presented with pallor, fatigue, reduced exercise tolerance, and exertional shortness of breath. His anemia persisted despite a month of oral iron supplementation.

THE TURNING POINT

A finding should never
be read in isolation.

Normal iron studies, persistent microcytosis, target cells, and abnormal hemoglobin findings challenged my initial assumption of iron deficiency. The case pointed toward a problem involving globin synthesis.

α-globin gene defect↓Reduced α-globin synthesis↓Globin-chain imbalance↓Ineffective erythropoiesis / hemolysis↓Reduced oxygen delivery↓The symptoms a patient feels.
“The chapters were no longer separate topics.
They were becoming one story.”
